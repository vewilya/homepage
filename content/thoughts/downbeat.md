The biggest challenge when learning a musical instrument these days is not necessarily technique or music. Besides a wealth of amazing professional musicians who teach, there's lots of content out there to help you with that. But music students often struggle with discipline, focus and consistency in their practice routines.

There are quite a few great apps for learning music and musical instruments: [Yousician](https://yousician.com), [Tomplay](https://tomplay.com) (a Swiss company with a great product), [Anytune](https://anytune.app), [BandLab](https://www.bandlab.com) and many more. But most of them focus on musical content and technical skills rather than on the practice process itself.

When we were tasked with coming up with an idea for a music companion app in our computer science studies at [HSLU Informatik](https://www.hslu.ch/informatik), we first talked to our HSLU music students. After evaluating what they had told us, we decided to focus on the practice process rather than on the technical details of learning an instrument.

We developed a prototype called Downbeat, a companion app that guides you through your daily practice. Downbeat helps you set goals, creates a practice plan for you, and gives you access to practice tools like a tuner, a metronome and timbre analysis. What the app doesn't do is provide professional musical feedback. In our book, that is still the job of a professional music teacher.
